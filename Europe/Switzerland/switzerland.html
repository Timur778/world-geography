Switzerland

Switzerland is a mountainous heart of Europe — defined by neutrality, precision, and decentralized governance, blending Alpine geography with global economic influence.

Region: Central Europe (Alpine)

Time Zone: UTC +1 (UTC +2 DST)

Population: ~8.9 million

Capital: Bern

Area: 41,285 km²

Language: German, French, Italian, Romansh

Currency: Swiss Franc (CHF)

Culture: Multilingual, federal & precision-driven

Alpine Landscape

The Alps dominate Switzerland’s geography, shaping transport, defense, and national identity.

Quick fact: About 60% of Switzerland is mountainous.

Early Confederation

Switzerland began as a defensive alliance of Alpine communities in the Middle Ages.

Neutrality

Permanent neutrality has defined Swiss foreign policy since 1815.

Quick fact: Switzerland did not join the UN until 2002.

Federal System

Strong cantonal autonomy forms the backbone of Swiss governance.

Direct Democracy

Citizens regularly vote on laws, budgets, and constitutional changes.

Economic Precision

Banking, pharmaceuticals, engineering, and high-value manufacturing drive the economy.

Global Mediation

Switzerland hosts international organizations and peace negotiations.

Quick fact: Geneva hosts the UN’s European headquarters.

Quality of Life

High income, safety, and infrastructure consistently rank Switzerland among the world’s best.

Environmental Care

Environmental protection and sustainable transport are national priorities.

European Position

Switzerland cooperates closely with the EU while remaining outside the union.

The Road Ahead

Switzerland’s future lies in balancing openness, neutrality, and innovation.
