Malta

Malta is a Mediterranean crossroads — small in size, massive in history — shaped by empires, sea power, and a strategic position that made it one of Europe’s most contested islands.

Region: Southern Europe (Mediterranean)

Time Zone: UTC +1 (UTC +2 DST)

Population: ~540,000

Capital: Valletta

Area: 316 km²

Language: Maltese & English

Currency: Euro (EUR)

Culture: Mediterranean roots & layered history

Island Geography

Malta is an archipelago in the central Mediterranean, lacking rivers or mountains but rich in natural harbors.

Quick fact: Malta has no permanent rivers or lakes.

Ancient Settlers

Malta hosts some of the world’s oldest free-standing stone temples, predating the pyramids.

Strategic Crossroads

Phoenicians, Romans, Arabs, and Normans all controlled Malta due to its naval importance.

Quick fact: Malta sat on key Mediterranean trade routes.

Knights of St. John

The Knights transformed Malta into a fortress state and naval stronghold.

British Rule

Malta became a key British naval base, especially during World War II.

World War II

Malta endured intense bombing and was awarded the George Cross for bravery.

Quick fact: The George Cross appears on Malta’s flag.

Independence

Malta gained independence in 1964 and became a republic in 1974.

Neutral State

Malta follows a policy of neutrality while remaining active in diplomacy.

Modern Economy

Finance, tourism, gaming, and logistics drive Malta’s modern economy.

Language & Identity

Maltese is the only Semitic language written in the Latin alphabet in Europe.

The Road Ahead

Malta’s future depends on managing growth, sustainability, and preserving its historical character.
