Czech Republic

The Czech Republic sits at the heart of Europe — a country shaped by medieval kingdoms, industrial strength, and a modern identity built on resilience, pragmatism, and culture.

Region: Central Europe

Time Zone: UTC +1 (UTC +2 DST)

Population: ~10.7 million

Capital: Prague

Area: 78,866 km²

Language: Czech

Currency: Czech Koruna (CZK)

Culture: Central European & civic-oriented

Bohemian Lands

The Czech lands historically include Bohemia, Moravia, and Czech Silesia, each contributing distinct traditions and identities.

Quick fact: Prague is nicknamed “the City of a Hundred Spires.”

Medieval Power

The Kingdom of Bohemia was a major political and cultural center within the Holy Roman Empire.

Religious Reform

The Czech lands were early centers of religious reform, most notably through Jan Hus, who influenced later European movements.

Habsburg Rule

Centuries under Habsburg control tied the region to Vienna while limiting political autonomy.

Industrial Strength

The Czech lands became one of Europe’s most industrialized regions by the 19th century.

Czechoslovakia

After World War I, Czechoslovakia emerged as a democratic state with strong industry and culture.

Quick fact: Czechoslovakia was founded in 1918.

Communist Period

Post-World War II communism reshaped politics, economy, and society for four decades.

Velvet Revolution

In 1989, peaceful protests ended communist rule and opened the path to democracy.

Quick fact: The Velvet Revolution was largely non-violent.

Peaceful Split

The Czech Republic emerged in 1993 after a peaceful separation from Slovakia.

Modern Czechia

Today’s Czech Republic combines strong manufacturing, technology, and a high quality of life.

The Road Ahead

The country’s future lies in innovation, civic stability, and maintaining its central role in Europe.
