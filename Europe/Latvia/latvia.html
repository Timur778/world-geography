Latvia

Latvia is a Baltic nation shaped by forests, trade routes, and foreign rule — resilient, culturally distinct, and firmly anchored in modern Europe after a turbulent history.

Region: Northern Europe (Baltic)

Time Zone: UTC +2 (UTC +3 DST)

Population: ~1.9 million

Capital: Riga

Area: 64,589 km²

Language: Latvian

Currency: Euro (EUR)

Culture: Baltic heritage & song traditions

Forests & Coast

Latvia is heavily forested with a long Baltic Sea coastline, shaping settlement and trade.

Quick fact: Over half of Latvia is covered by forests.

Ancient Balts

Latvian identity traces back to Baltic tribes who lived in the region long before medieval conquest.

Medieval Trade

Riga became a major Hanseatic League city, connecting Northern Europe through commerce.

Foreign Rule

German, Polish, Swedish, and Russian powers controlled Latvian lands over centuries.

First Independence

Latvia declared independence in 1918 following World War I.

Quick fact: Latvia became independent in 1918.

Soviet Occupation

Soviet rule deeply altered demographics, economy, and political life.

Restored Statehood

Latvia regained independence in 1991 as the Soviet Union collapsed.

European Integration

Latvia joined the EU and NATO, anchoring its security and economy in the West.

Culture & Song

Mass song and dance festivals are central expressions of national identity.

Modern Latvia

Latvia balances digital innovation with preserving language, culture, and sovereignty.

The Road Ahead

Latvia’s future depends on demographic stability, economic growth, and regional security.
