Spain

Spain is a nation of layered civilizations and strong regional identities — shaped by empires, faith, and culture, and defined today by diversity, resilience, and modern European life.

Region: Southern Europe (Iberian Peninsula)

Time Zone: UTC +1 (UTC +2 DST)

Population: ~48 million

Capital: Madrid

Area: 505,990 km²

Language: Spanish (Castilian)

Currency: Euro (EUR)

Culture: Regional diversity & Mediterranean heritage

Geographic Diversity

Spain features mountains, plateaus, fertile plains, and long Mediterranean and Atlantic coastlines.

Quick fact: Spain has the second-highest mountain range in Europe after the Alps.

Ancient Civilizations

Iberians, Celts, Phoenicians, and Romans shaped early Spanish history.

Islamic Al-Andalus

Muslim rule introduced advanced science, architecture, and agriculture.

Quick fact: Córdoba was once Europe’s largest city.

Reconquista

Christian kingdoms gradually reclaimed the peninsula by 1492.

Global Empire

Spain built one of the largest empires in history during the 16th century.

Decline & Reform

Economic strain and conflict weakened imperial power over time.

Civil War

The Spanish Civil War (1936–1939) deeply divided the nation.

Franco Era

Decades of authoritarian rule followed until the mid-1970s.

Democratic Transition

Spain transitioned peacefully to democracy after 1975.

Quick fact: Spain’s 1978 constitution established a constitutional monarchy.

Regional Identity

Autonomous regions play a central role in politics and culture.

The Road Ahead

Spain’s future depends on economic resilience, unity, and social cohesion.
