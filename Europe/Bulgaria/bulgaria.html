Bulgaria

Bulgaria is one of Europe’s oldest states — a crossroads of Thracian, Slavic, and Byzantine worlds, balancing ancient continuity with modern transformation.

Region: Southeast Europe (Balkans)

Time Zone: UTC +2 (UTC +3 DST)

Population: ~6.4 million

Capital: Sofia

Area: 110,879 km²

Language: Bulgarian

Currency: Bulgarian Lev (BGN)

Culture: Slavic heritage & Orthodox traditions

Ancient Thrace

Long before Slavs arrived, Thracian civilizations dominated the region, leaving rich archaeological and cultural heritage.

Quick fact: Bulgaria has some of the world’s oldest gold treasures.

Birth of Bulgaria

The Bulgarian state was founded in the 7th century, making it one of the oldest continuously named countries in Europe.

Quick fact: The name “Bulgaria” has existed since 681 AD.

Medieval Empires

The First and Second Bulgarian Empires rivaled Byzantium in power, culture, and military strength.

Cyrillic Alphabet

Bulgaria played a central role in developing and spreading the Cyrillic script across Eastern Europe.

Quick fact: Cyrillic is now used by over 250 million people worldwide.

Ottoman Rule

Nearly five centuries of Ottoman control reshaped Bulgarian society, religion, and architecture.

National Revival

The 19th-century revival reignited Bulgarian language, education, and identity.

Modern Independence

Bulgaria regained independence in the late 19th century and navigated wars, monarchies, and shifting alliances.

Quick fact: Bulgaria gained independence from the Ottoman Empire in 1908.

Communist Era

Post-WWII Bulgaria became a socialist state, industrializing rapidly under centralized control.

EU Membership

Joining the European Union in 2007 marked a major shift toward integration and reform.

Culture & Traditions

Folk music, dances, yogurt, rose oil, and seasonal rituals remain core elements of Bulgarian life.

The Road Ahead

Bulgaria’s future depends on economic modernization, demographic stability, and preserving its deep cultural roots.
