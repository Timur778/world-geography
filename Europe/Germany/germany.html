Germany

Germany is Europe’s industrial and political engine — a country defined by discipline, innovation, and a hard-earned commitment to democratic stability after a turbulent past.

Region: Central Europe

Time Zone: UTC +1 (UTC +2 DST)

Population: ~84 million

Capital: Berlin

Area: 357,588 km²

Language: German

Currency: Euro (EUR)

Culture: Order, innovation & civic responsibility

Geographic Core

Germany lies at the heart of Europe, connecting north and south, east and west through rivers, plains, and transport corridors.

Quick fact: Germany shares borders with nine countries.

Holy Roman Empire

For centuries, German lands formed the fragmented Holy Roman Empire, shaping Europe’s political complexity.

Reformation

Martin Luther’s Protestant Reformation began in Germany, permanently changing European religion and politics.

Quick fact: The Reformation began in 1517.

Late Unification

Germany unified as a modern nation only in 1871, quickly becoming a major industrial and military power.

World Wars

Germany’s role in both World Wars led to devastation, division, and deep national reckoning.

Division & Cold War

After 1945, Germany was split into East and West, symbolized by the Berlin Wall.

Quick fact: The Berlin Wall fell in 1989.

Reunification

German reunification in 1990 reshaped Europe and restored national unity.

Economic Engine

Germany leads Europe in manufacturing, engineering, and exports, anchored by the Mittelstand.

Political Influence

Germany plays a central role in the European Union’s economic and political direction.

Culture & Society

Philosophy, music, science, and a strong civic culture remain core to German identity.

The Road Ahead

Germany’s future depends on innovation, energy transition, and maintaining democratic resilience in a changing world.
