Greece

Greece is the birthplace of Western civilization — a country where ancient philosophy, democracy, and art laid foundations that still shape the modern world.

Region: Southern Europe

Time Zone: UTC +2 (UTC +3 DST)

Population: ~10.3 million

Capital: Athens

Area: 131,957 km²

Language: Greek

Currency: Euro (EUR)

Culture: Ancient heritage & Mediterranean life

Mountain & Sea

Greece combines rugged mountains with one of the longest coastlines in Europe, scattered with thousands of islands.

Quick fact: Greece has over 6,000 islands and islets.

Ancient Greece

City-states like Athens and Sparta shaped politics, philosophy, science, and warfare in the ancient world.

Birth of Democracy

Athens introduced early democratic governance, influencing political systems across history.

Quick fact: Democracy emerged in Athens in the 5th century BC.

Hellenistic World

Alexander the Great expanded Greek culture across three continents.

Roman & Byzantine Era

Greece became part of the Roman and later Byzantine empires, preserving classical knowledge.

Ottoman Rule

Centuries of Ottoman control shaped society, religion, and resistance movements.

Modern Independence

Greece gained independence in the 19th century after a long struggle.

Quick fact: Greece became independent in 1830.

20th Century Turmoil

Wars, occupation, dictatorship, and recovery defined modern Greek history.

EU Membership

Greece joined the European Union in 1981, integrating economically and politically.

Culture & Life

Greek culture values family, food, philosophy, music, and communal life.

The Road Ahead

Greece’s future depends on economic resilience, sustainability, and preserving its unparalleled cultural legacy.
