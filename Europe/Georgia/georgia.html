Georgia

Georgia sits at the crossroads of Europe and Asia — a mountainous country shaped by ancient kingdoms, strategic trade routes, and a strong cultural identity forged through survival.

Region: Eastern Europe / Caucasus

Time Zone: UTC +4

Population: ~3.7 million

Capital: Tbilisi

Area: 69,700 km²

Language: Georgian

Currency: Georgian Lari (GEL)

Culture: Ancient traditions & Caucasus resilience

Caucasus Mountains

Georgia is dominated by the Greater and Lesser Caucasus ranges, shaping settlement, defense, and culture.

Quick fact: Mount Shkhara is Georgia’s highest peak.

Ancient Kingdoms

Early Georgian states like Colchis and Iberia played roles in regional trade and politics thousands of years ago.

Early Christianity

Georgia adopted Christianity in the 4th century, becoming one of the world’s earliest Christian nations.

Quick fact: Georgia adopted Christianity in 337 AD.

Golden Age

The medieval Georgian kingdom reached its peak under Queen Tamar, expanding influence and culture.

Foreign Domination

Mongol, Persian, and Ottoman pressures weakened Georgia over centuries, fragmenting the kingdom.

Russian Empire

In the 19th century, Georgia was absorbed into the Russian Empire, reshaping governance and borders.

Soviet Period

As a Soviet republic, Georgia industrialized while facing political repression and cultural control.

Modern Independence

Georgia regained independence in 1991, navigating conflict, reform, and geopolitical pressure.

Quick fact: Georgia became independent in 1991.

Culture & Identity

Georgian culture emphasizes hospitality, polyphonic music, dance, and one of the world’s oldest wine traditions.

Strategic Position

Georgia remains strategically important as a bridge between Europe, Central Asia, and the Middle East.

The Road Ahead

Georgia’s future depends on stability, reform, and balancing regional pressures with European aspirations.
