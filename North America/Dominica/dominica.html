Dominica

Dominica is a country defined by nature first — a mountainous, rain-soaked island where forests, rivers, and volcanoes shape daily life more than cities ever could.

Region: Caribbean (North America)

Time Zone: UTC −4

Population: ~70,000

Capital: Roseau

Area: 751 km²

Language: English

Currency: East Caribbean Dollar

Culture: Environmental identity & resilience

Indigenous Roots

Dominica is home to the Kalinago people, the last surviving Indigenous population in the Eastern Caribbean, whose presence continues to shape national identity.

Quick fact: Dominica is the only Caribbean island with a recognized Indigenous territory.

Volcanic Landscape

Steep mountains, boiling lakes, and countless rivers define the island, limiting large-scale settlement and preserving vast wilderness.

Colonial Struggles

French and British powers competed for control, but Dominica’s terrain made full domination difficult for centuries.

Late Development

Unlike plantation-heavy islands, Dominica developed smaller-scale agriculture and avoided extreme urban concentration.

Independence

Gaining independence in 1978, Dominica faced early economic and political challenges while building national institutions.

Quick fact: Dominica is a republic, not a constitutional monarchy.

Economy & Nature

Agriculture, eco-tourism, and renewable energy projects define the economy, tightly linked to environmental preservation.

Climate Vulnerability

Hurricanes have caused repeated devastation, pushing Dominica to reimagine itself as a climate-resilient nation.

Culture & Community

Village life, music, and festivals emphasize cooperation and shared recovery after natural disasters.

Global Voice

Dominica advocates strongly on climate issues, using its experience to influence international environmental discussions.

The Road Ahead

Dominica’s future lies in resilience — protecting its ecosystems while adapting infrastructure and livelihoods to a changing climate.
