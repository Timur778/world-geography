St. Martin

St. Martin is a single island with a split identity — European governance, Caribbean rhythm, and a daily life shaped by borders that exist without walls.

Region: Caribbean (North America)

Time Zone: UTC −4

Population: ~77,000

Capital: Marigot

Area: 87 km²

Language: French, English, Dutch

Currency: Euro

Culture: Dual governance & island fusion

Pre-Colonial Roots

Indigenous Arawak peoples inhabited the island long before European arrival, relying on fishing and trade.

Quick fact: The island was sighted by Christopher Columbus in 1493.

A Divided Island

St. Martin is uniquely shared between France and the Netherlands, divided peacefully since the 17th century.

Colonial Competition

Salt production and trade attracted European powers, shaping settlement and governance.

Border Without Barriers

Residents cross the internal border freely, creating a blended social and economic space.

Quick fact: You can cross the border dozens of times a day without noticing.

Tourism Economy

Tourism dominates the economy, driven by beaches, duty-free shopping, and cruise traffic.

Hurricane Impact

Severe storms periodically disrupt life, testing infrastructure and recovery systems.

Multicultural Life

Migration from across the Caribbean and Europe created a multilingual, multicultural population.

Governance

As a French overseas collectivity, St. Martin follows European law while retaining local autonomy.

Regional Role

The island acts as a connector between Caribbean tourism networks and Europe.

The Road Ahead

St. Martin’s future depends on climate resilience, economic balance, and preserving its shared identity.
