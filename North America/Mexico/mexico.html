Mexico

Mexico is a civilization layered in motion — ancient worlds, colonial power, and modern industry overlapping in a country that never fully erased its past.

Region: North America

Time Zone: UTC −5 to −8

Population: ~129 million

Capital: Mexico City

Area: 1.96M km²

Language: Spanish (plus Indigenous languages)

Currency: Mexican Peso

Culture: Indigenous continuity & national identity

Ancient Civilizations

Mexico was home to some of the most advanced civilizations of the ancient world, including the Olmec, Maya, and Aztec, whose cities, calendars, and cosmology shaped the region.

Quick fact: Mexico City is built on the ruins of the Aztec capital, Tenochtitlán.

Geography & Diversity

Deserts, rainforests, mountains, and long coastlines divide Mexico into distinct cultural and economic regions.

Spanish Conquest

The Spanish conquest reorganized land, religion, and power, creating a blended society rather than replacing Indigenous culture entirely.

Colonial Legacy

Mining and centralized administration tied Mexico to global trade while deepening social hierarchies.

Independence & Revolution

Independence in the 19th century and revolution in the early 20th century reshaped land ownership, labor rights, and national identity.

Quick fact: The Mexican Revolution began in 1910.

Urban Power

Mexico City grew into one of the world’s largest megacities, concentrating political, cultural, and economic power.

Modern Economy

Manufacturing, trade, and services integrate Mexico tightly into global supply chains, especially with North America.

Cultural Expression

Food, art, music, and ritual remain living expressions of identity rather than preserved tradition.

Social Challenges

Inequality, migration, and security concerns continue to shape public debate and policy.

The Road Ahead

Mexico’s future lies in balancing growth with inclusion, honoring ancient roots while navigating modern pressures.
