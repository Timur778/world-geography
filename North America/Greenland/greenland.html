Greenland

Greenland is a land of extremes — vast ice, minimal population, and a way of life shaped more by survival and nature than by borders or power.

Region: Arctic (North America)

Time Zone: UTC −4 to −1

Population: ~56,000

Capital: Nuuk

Area: 2.16M km²

Language: Greenlandic & Danish

Currency: Danish Krone

Culture: Inuit heritage & Arctic adaptation

Indigenous Origins

Greenland has been inhabited for thousands of years by Inuit peoples who adapted to Arctic life through hunting, fishing, and deep environmental knowledge.

Quick fact: Inuit culture remains the dominant cultural influence in Greenland.

Ice & Geography

Over 80% of Greenland is covered by an ice sheet, limiting settlement to coastal areas and shaping transportation and trade.

Norse Settlement

Viking settlers arrived around the 10th century, establishing colonies that eventually disappeared due to climate and isolation.

Danish Rule

Greenland became part of the Danish realm, influencing governance, language, and economic systems.

Quick fact: Greenland gained self-rule from Denmark in 1979.

Modern Autonomy

Today, Greenland governs most of its internal affairs while Denmark manages defense and foreign policy.

Climate Change

Melting ice is transforming landscapes, ecosystems, and global sea levels, placing Greenland at the center of climate research.

Economy

Fishing dominates the economy, with emerging interest in mining and renewable energy.

Strategic Interest

Greenland’s location and resources have drawn growing global attention in Arctic geopolitics.

Identity & Language

Cultural revival emphasizes Greenlandic language, tradition, and national self-determination.

The Road Ahead

Greenland’s future balances autonomy, environmental change, and sustainable development in one of Earth’s harshest environments.
