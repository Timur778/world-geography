Grenada

Grenada is a small island with a loud history — a country where spice, revolution, and resilience shaped an identity far larger than its size.

Region: Caribbean (North America)

Time Zone: UTC −4

Population: ~125,000

Capital: St. George’s

Area: 344 km²

Language: English

Currency: East Caribbean Dollar

Culture: Spice heritage & community life

Early Inhabitants

Indigenous peoples lived on the island long before European arrival, relying on fishing and small-scale agriculture.

Quick fact: Grenada was originally inhabited by the Kalinago people.

Volcanic Island

Grenada’s volcanic origins created fertile soil, supporting dense vegetation and agricultural development.

Colonial Rivalry

France and Britain fought for control of the island, leaving cultural and legal influences that still coexist.

Spice Economy

Nutmeg, cinnamon, and cocoa defined Grenada’s economy, earning it the nickname “Spice Isle.”

Quick fact: Grenada is one of the world’s largest nutmeg exporters.

Political Upheaval

The late 20th century brought revolution and foreign intervention, deeply impacting national politics and identity.

Independence

Grenada gained independence in 1974, navigating early instability while building democratic institutions.

Modern Economy

Tourism, agriculture, and education services now drive growth, supported by regional cooperation.

Culture & Music

Carnival, calypso, and strong village ties reinforce social cohesion and shared memory.

Environmental Risk

Hurricanes and climate change threaten agriculture and coastal infrastructure.

The Road Ahead

Grenada’s future depends on economic diversification, climate resilience, and preserving its agricultural heritage.
