Zimbabwe

Zimbabwe is a southern African country shaped by ancient civilizations, rich landscapes, and modern political change.

Region: Southern Africa

Time Zone: UTC +2

Population: ~16 million

Capital: Harare

Area: 390,757 km²

Language: English

Currency: Zimbabwean dollar

Culture: Shona-Ndebele

Plateau Geography

Zimbabwe lies on a high plateau with savannas, river valleys, and granite landscapes.

Rivers such as the Zambezi and Limpopo define natural borders.

Great Zimbabwe

The ancient city of Great Zimbabwe was a major center of trade and power between the 11th and 15th centuries.

Its stone architecture reflects advanced engineering and governance.

Pre-Colonial Trade

Gold, ivory, and cattle connected Zimbabwe to Indian Ocean trade networks.

Regional influence extended far beyond modern borders.

Colonial Rule

Britain colonized the region as Southern Rhodesia.

Settler policies reshaped land ownership and economy.

Independence

Zimbabwe gained independence in 1980 after a prolonged liberation struggle.

The new nation focused on education and social development.

Quick fact: Zimbabwe became independent in 1980.

Economic Challenges

Land reform and political decisions triggered economic instability.

Hyperinflation reshaped everyday life.

Culture & Identity

Shona and Ndebele cultures emphasize music, spirituality, and oral tradition.

Stone carving and mbira music are internationally recognized.

Quick fact: Mbira music is central to spiritual ceremonies.

Modern Zimbabwe

Zimbabwe continues to rebuild its economy and institutions.

Agriculture and mining remain key sectors.

The Road Ahead

Zimbabwe’s future depends on economic reform and political stability.

Harnessing human capital remains critical.
