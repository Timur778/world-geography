Senegal

Senegal is a West African country shaped by Atlantic trade, Islamic scholarship, and cultural influence across the region.

Region: West Africa

Time Zone: UTC +0

Population: ~18 million

Capital: Dakar

Area: 196,722 km²

Language: French

Currency: CFA franc

Culture: Wolof

Atlantic Geography

Senegal sits on the Atlantic coast at the western edge of Africa, shaping its role in trade, migration, and cultural exchange.

Rivers such as the Senegal and Casamance support agriculture and settlement.

Early Kingdoms

Pre-colonial states such as Tekrur and Jolof shaped political organization and trade.

These kingdoms connected inland societies to coastal commerce.

Islamic Scholarship

Islam spread gradually through trade and education, becoming central to Senegalese identity.

Religious brotherhoods played a key role in social cohesion.

French Colonization

France established Senegal as a colonial hub in West Africa.

Dakar became a major administrative and port city.

Independence

Senegal gained independence in 1960 and maintained relative political stability.

Democratic institutions developed gradually.

Quick fact: Senegal became independent in 1960.

Political Culture

Senegal is known for peaceful political transitions and civic engagement.

Dialogue remains central to governance.

Culture & Identity

Wolof culture influences language, music, and everyday life nationwide.

Hospitality and community values are deeply rooted.

Quick fact: Dakar is a major cultural capital of West Africa.

Modern Senegal

Senegal’s economy is diversifying into services, energy, and infrastructure.

Urban growth reshapes Dakar and regional cities.

The Road Ahead

Senegal’s future depends on inclusive growth and youth employment.

Maintaining stability remains a national strength.
