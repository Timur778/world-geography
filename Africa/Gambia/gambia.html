The Gambia

The Gambia is a narrow West African country shaped by the Gambia River, trade, and a strong communal culture.

Region: West Africa

Time Zone: UTC +0

Population: ~2.7 million

Capital: Banjul

Area: 11,295 km²

Language: English

Currency: Dalasi

Culture: Mandé

River Geography

The Gambia follows the course of the Gambia River, which runs from the interior of West Africa to the Atlantic Ocean.

This river has shaped settlement, agriculture, and trade, making it the country’s central lifeline.

Early Kingdoms

The region was influenced by powerful West African empires such as Mali, which shaped trade, culture, and governance.

River-based commerce connected local communities to broader regional networks.

European Contact

Portuguese and later British traders arrived along the river, integrating the area into Atlantic trade systems.

British control eventually formalized the colony.

Colonial Rule

Under British administration, the economy focused on peanuts and river trade.

Colonial borders left The Gambia surrounded almost entirely by Senegal.

Independence

The Gambia gained independence in 1965 and initially developed as a stable parliamentary democracy.

Small size encouraged diplomatic cooperation.

Quick fact: The Gambia became independent in 1965.

Political Change

Periods of authoritarian rule shaped politics in the late 20th century.

Recent years have focused on democratic transition and reform.

Culture & Identity

“Mandé” culture reflects strong traditions of oral history, music, and communal life.

Hospitality and social cohesion are deeply valued.

Quick fact: Griots play a key role in preserving history.

Modern Economy

The economy relies on agriculture, tourism, and remittances.

Coastal tourism has become increasingly important.

The Road Ahead

The Gambia’s future depends on democratic consolidation and economic diversification.

Managing resources sustainably will shape long-term resilience.
