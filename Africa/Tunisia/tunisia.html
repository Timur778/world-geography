Tunisia

Tunisia is a North African country shaped by Mediterranean trade, ancient civilizations, and modern reform.

Region: North Africa

Time Zone: UTC +1

Population: ~12 million

Capital: Tunis

Area: 163,610 km²

Language: Arabic

Currency: Tunisian dinar

Culture: Maghrebi

Mediterranean Geography

Tunisia sits between the Mediterranean Sea and the Sahara Desert, creating sharp contrasts in climate and settlement.

Fertile coastal plains supported agriculture and dense urban life.

Carthaginian Legacy

Ancient Carthage emerged as a major maritime power rivaling Rome.

Trade networks extended across the Mediterranean world.

Roman Africa

Under Roman rule, Tunisia became a breadbasket of the empire.

Amphitheaters, roads, and cities still mark the landscape.

Islamic Era

Arab-Islamic expansion integrated Tunisia into the Islamic world.

Kairouan became a center of scholarship and religious learning.

Ottoman & French Rule

Tunisia was part of the Ottoman Empire before becoming a French protectorate.

Colonial rule modernized administration and infrastructure.

Independence

Tunisia gained independence in 1956 and pursued state-led modernization.

Education and women’s rights became national priorities.

Quick fact: Tunisia gained independence in 1956.

Culture & Identity

Maghrebi culture blends Amazigh roots with Arab and Mediterranean influence.

Cuisine, music, and urban life reflect layered history.

Quick fact: Kairouan is one of Islam’s holiest cities.

Modern Tunisia

Tunisia balances secular governance with cultural tradition.

Tourism and manufacturing support the economy.

The Road Ahead

Tunisia’s future depends on economic reform and political stability.

Youth opportunity remains a central issue.
